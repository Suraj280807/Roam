ABSTRACT

Roam — AI-Powered Travel Companion is a modern, responsive web application engineered to transform standard geographic navigation into an interactive journey of cultural discovery. Traditional navigation systems focus strictly on turn-by-turn routing between coordinates, often leaving travelers disconnected from the historical, cultural, and architectural significance of the places they encounter. Roam bridges this divide by providing an integrated, client-first digital travel companion built with React 19, TypeScript, TanStack Router, Leaflet, and Tailwind CSS.

The application incorporates five core exploration capabilities: real-time proximity discovery using the mathematical Haversine formula, an interactive mapping interface with dynamic category filters, a browser-native voice audio tour narrator utilizing the Web Speech Synthesis API, a landmark photo capture interface with multi-tier client-side image compression, and a gamified Digital Travel Passport enabling travelers to collect digital stamps, earn exploration coins, and unlock regional partner discounts.

The system is designed with a lightweight, client-focused architecture. Personal notes, stamps and journal data are stored locally in the browser. Location resolution and map display may communicate with external services when required. The project encompasses field work and data verification conducted in the Sanjay Ghodawat University (SGU) campus and Hatkanangle/Kolhapur heritage region, complemented by a curated baseline demonstration pack. Roam provides students, tourists, and independent explorers with an engaging and educational travel experience.

Keywords : Travel Companion, Geolocation, Haversine Distance, Web Speech API, Landmark Photo Capture, Digital Passport, React 19, TypeScript.

1. INTRODUCTION

Modern global tourism and daily commuting rely heavily on mobile navigation platforms. While existing map applications excel at calculating the shortest turn-by-turn route between geographic coordinates, they treat journeys primarily as logistical transit. As travelers move from one location to another, they frequently pass ancient temples, historic fortresses, prominent educational institutions, viewpoints, and artisanal local businesses without discovering why these places matter or understanding their cultural heritage.

Our project, Roam — AI-Powered Travel Companion, is a client-first, responsive web application engineered to transform passive geographic navigation into an engaging journey of interactive exploration. Built on modern web standards using React 19, TypeScript, TanStack Router, Leaflet, and Tailwind CSS, Roam acts as a personal intelligent guide that accompanies travelers wherever they explore.

The core philosophy of Roam is encapsulated in a five-stage exploration paradigm: Discover → Explore → Learn → Earn → Remember. Rather than leaving travelers to juggle separate mapping utilities, web searches, guidebooks, and photo albums, Roam integrates real-time proximity discovery, historical storytelling, voice audio narration, camera-based landmark photo capture, digital passport gamification, and personal travel journaling into a unified browser experience.

The scope of the project focuses on responsive client-side intelligence, HTML5 geolocation with automatic IP fallback, client-side canvas image compression, and reliable local state management. Personal notes, stamps and journal data are stored locally in the browser. Location resolution and map display may communicate with external services when required. The system operates with transparent mathematical distance formulas (Haversine equation) and browser-native Speech Synthesis.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW / EXISTING SYSTEM
2.1 Background study

Contemporary spatial navigation has evolved dramatically over the past two decades. Early digital navigation relied on static pre-rendered roadmaps and standalone GPS receivers. The advent of modern mapping APIs, such as Google Maps, Mapbox, and OpenStreetMap, revolutionized mobile navigation by enabling real-time GPS coordinate mapping, dynamic routing, and estimated arrival times. However, these systems are fundamentally optimized for vehicular traffic and point-to-point transit efficiency rather than leisurely foot exploration or cultural discovery.

In parallel, digital tourism platforms such as TripAdvisor, Lonely Planet, and Wikivoyage provide comprehensive crowd-sourced reviews, ratings, and travel itineraries. While these platforms contain extensive catalogs of popular attractions, their user interfaces are dense, text-heavy, and heavily monetized with third-party advertisements and affiliate booking links. Travelers are required to switch continuously between mapping tools to locate a site and review websites to learn about it, resulting in fragmented user experiences.

Dedicated audio tour mobile applications, such as VoiceMap and PocketGuide, offer GPS-triggered spoken walking tours. However, they typically rely on proprietary, heavy native mobile downloads, expensive paywalled content packs, and rigid linear itineraries that penalize spontaneous exploration. Furthermore, emerging mobile applications with augmented reality (AR) or machine-learning object recognition often demand intensive GPU computation and continuous cloud API roundtrips that consume significant mobile data and deplete battery life rapidly.

In recent years, gamified exploration concepts (such as Geocaching and location-based quest games) have demonstrated that achievement mechanisms significantly boost user motivation to explore local environments. Nevertheless, few systems seamlessly unify lightweight web-based audio storytelling, spatial proximity calculations, and collectible digital passport stamps within a zero-install, privacy-first single-page application.

3. MOTIVATION, PROBLEM STATEMENT AND OBJECTIVES
3.1 Motivation

Travel has the unique ability to broaden horizons, foster cultural empathy, and connect individuals to history. However, in our fast-paced digital era, smartphones often detach users from their immediate surroundings. Travelers navigate between points staring at directional arrows, missing the rich heritage beneath their feet. In historical regions like Kolhapur and vibrant campuses like Sanjay Ghodawat University, visitors often walk past sites of architectural and cultural significance without awareness of their background.

As undergraduate students in Computer Science and Engineering, our primary motivation was to harness modern web engineering to bridge this gap. We sought to build an application that does not merely tell a user where to go, but reveals why a place matters. From an engineering standpoint, this project provided an ideal opportunity to integrate cutting-edge single-page application (SPA) paradigms with React 19, TypeScript, TanStack Router, Leaflet mapping, browser hardware APIs (Geolocation, Speech Synthesis, Camera), and responsive UI design.

3.2 Problem statement

Modern digital navigation and tourism tools remain severely fragmented. Traditional mapping tools provide geometric routing without cultural depth; travel guide platforms present cumbersome, ad-laden articles; audio guides require expensive proprietary apps; and memorable travel reflections remain scattered across disconnected social media albums. Furthermore, travelers frequently encounter poor internet connectivity and restrictive device permissions while in the field.

The problem addressed by this project is to design, implement, and validate Roam: an integrated, lightweight, client-first web companion that automatically identifies user proximity to landmarks, delivers hands-free voice audio storytelling, provides a landmark photo capture and compression interface, gamifies exploration through digital stamps and merchant rewards, and records personal travel memories—all operating smoothly across desktop and mobile browsers.

4
3.3 Objectives
The central aim of the project is to construct a production-ready, client-first web application that transforms travel navigation into an interactive learning journey. To accomplish this, the specific objectives are formulated as follows:
  1. Proximity Geolocation Engine: To implement a real-time location service that computes precise distances between the user and cataloged landmarks using the mathematical Haversine formula.
  2. Multi-Tier Geolocation Fallback: To engineer a resilient location resolution pipeline incorporating HTML5 high-accuracy GPS, Google Maps reverse geocoding, and automatic IP-based geolocation fallback when GPS permissions are withheld.
  3. Interactive Map Visualization: To integrate Leaflet-based interactive maps displaying categorized, color-coded pins, custom popups, and spatial exploration boundaries.
  4. Browser-Native Audio Narration: To construct a hands-free audio tour storyteller leveraging the Web Speech Synthesis API with natural cadence and dedicated audio player controls.
  5. Landmark Photo Capture Interface: To implement a client-side photo capture interface featuring multi-tier canvas image compression (createObjectURL → FileReader → raw fallback) for mobile storage efficiency.
  6. Gamified Digital Travel Passport: To design a collectible stamp system where users unlock location-verified passport stamps and advance through progressive explorer tiers.
  7. Local Merchant Loyalty Integration: To create a partner coupon and quest reward mechanism linking landmark visits to real local businesses (such as The Tea Toast Co. and Cafe Amigos).
  8. Personal Travel Journal: To develop a private client journal allowing travelers to record date-stamped reflections, attach compressed photo memories, and manage saved landmarks.
  9. Privacy-Conscious Local State: To store personal notes, stamps, and journal reflections locally in the browser using React Context and LocalStorage, while external network communication is restricted to location resolution and map tiles.
  10. Comprehensive Boundary and Usability Testing: To conduct rigorous unit, boundary, and cross-browser testing across desktop and mobile devices under diverse network and permission scenarios.

4. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
4.1 Architecture overview
Roam is structured around a modern client-first, single-page application (SPA) architecture. The application is hosted as a static web bundle and executes entirely within the user's web browser, directly interacting with standardized HTML5 device hardware APIs. This approach eliminates server roundtrip latency for interactive exploration, ensures user data privacy for personal notes and collected stamps, and facilitates smooth offline recovery.
Figure 1: System architecture and request-response flow
4.2 Layers of the architecture
The architecture comprises three primary tiers: 1. Presentation Layer: Built with React 19, Tailwind CSS, Lucide icons, and Radix UI components. It renders responsive cards, modal sheets, and interactive Leaflet map layers. 2. Application & Logic Layer: Contains the central React Context state manager, TanStack Router routing tree, Haversine proximity computation, speech synthesis controller, and multi-tier image compression routines. 3. Service & Storage Layer: Interfaces with hardware APIs (HTML5 Geolocation, Web Speech API, MediaDevices Camera), external Google Maps geocoding endpoints, and persistent browser LocalStorage.

6. METHODOLOGY
6.1 Development approach
Roam was developed using an iterative Agile engineering lifecycle. Development commenced with requirements elicitation, identifying essential user workflows: proximity discovery, historical narration, landmark photo capture, gamified stamp collection, and journal reflections. We then implemented the core mathematical utilities (Haversine spherical distance), integrated the HTML5 Geolocation API with graceful IP fallback, and established the type-safe routing architecture using TanStack Router. Visual components were iteratively designed and validated using Tailwind CSS v4 and Radix primitives.
6.2 Working of the system
The application executes in the user's web browser. Upon launching, Roam requests geographic coordinates via the browser's Geolocation API. If granted, the system resolves real-time latitude and longitude, invokes the Google Maps Geocoding API to identify the locality (e.g., Atigre / Kolhapur), and computes distances to cataloged landmarks. If GPS permission is withheld, the system activates an automatic IP-based geolocation fallback or defaults to the curated regional pack. When the user selects a landmark, the system renders historical narratives, cultural significance, and an audio storyteller powered by the Web Speech API. Visiting a destination enables users to collect a digital stamp, earn exploration coins, and unlock merchant discount coupons.
6.3 Client state and modular architecture
The application utilizes React Context (StoreContext) for global state management, combined with HTML5 LocalStorage for persistence across browser reloads. The state store tracks user identity, collected stamps, personal journal entries, photo memories, exploration coins, and unlocked coupons. Hardware services—including the speech synthesis engine and canvas image compressor—are encapsulated in dedicated, decoupled modules (audio.ts, image.ts, location.ts) to preserve separation of concerns and enable isolated testing.

(evaluated when W > H) 		Constrains max dimension to 1,200 px at 0.75 JPEG compression quality.

6.5 Data handling and validation
Data integrity is safeguarded through structured Zod schema parsing and runtime boundary assertions. Geographic coordinates are validated to ensure −90.0° ≤ lat ≤ +90.0° and −180.0° ≤ lng ≤ +180.0°. Uploaded images are checked for valid MIME types (image/jpeg, image/png, image/webp) and dimension bounds prior to canvas processing. All localStorage reads are wrapped in defensive try-catch blocks with JSON schema fallback recovery to guard against corrupted client cache.
6.6 Testing approach
The application was verified using Vitest for automated unit and routing tests, combined with empirical end-to-end device trials. Test suites validate route matching, coordinate transformations, image compression fallbacks, and boundary error conditions.

9. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE
9.1 Conclusion

The Roam — AI-Powered Travel Companion project demonstrates how modern web engineering paradigms can elevate ordinary travel navigation into an engaging, culturally immersive journey. By integrating React 19, TypeScript, TanStack Router, Leaflet, and standard browser hardware APIs, the project delivers a comprehensive single-page application that unifies real-time proximity discovery, historical storytelling, voice narration, landmark photo capture, and gamified passport rewards.

Developing this project significantly enhanced our team's proficiency in component-driven frontend architecture, spherical geodesic calculations (Haversine equation), hardware API abstraction (Web Speech and HTML5 Geolocation), client-side canvas image optimization, and defensive validation. The successful deployment and field validation at Sanjay Ghodawat University and surrounding Kolhapur landmarks validate Roam as a practical, lightweight, and engaging exploration platform.

9.2 Future scope
The Roam platform offers strong potential for future architectural extensions:
  1. Progressive Web App (PWA) Offline Caching: Implementing service workers and IndexedDB to enable complete offline map browsing and audio guide playback in remote areas.
  2. Multi-Lingual Audio Narration: Expanding speech synthesis to support regional languages including Marathi and Hindi alongside English.
  3. Generative AI Interactive Q&A: Integrating secure on-device or edge LLM backends for dynamic historical inquiries.
  4. Augmented Reality (AR) Overlays: Adding WebXR camera overlays showing historical architectural reconstructions in real time.
  5. Expanded Merchant Loyalty Network: Onboarding additional regional artisans, historical museum ticketing, and eco-tourism partners.
